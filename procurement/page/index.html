Procurement on Kusama Asset Hub

Bid on a tender

A tender is one contract for one piece of work. It publishes what is wanted. Anyone can offer to do it at a price, tied to a document setting out what they will deliver. On a sealed tender, nobody sees a price, or the budget, until bidding has closed and the quality scores are fixed. The buyer then awards the work, and payment is held and released milestone by milestone.

Unfunded demonstration. This tender is tied to a placeholder token named "NOT dUSD" with no supply, so it can never receive or pay out money. A bid records your price and terms on Kusama Asset Hub and does nothing else. The contracts have not been independently reviewed.

How it runs

  1. Sealed bidding. Each bidder records a seal on chain: a hash of their price, their offer and a secret. Nobody, the buyer included, can see a price. The budget is sealed the same way when the tender is opened.
  2. Quality scoring. Bids close. Bidders publish their offers, which leave the price out. The buyer scores experience, method and dates and fixes a hash of those scores on chain, once. Prices are still closed.
  3. Prices open. Each bidder opens their own seal. The contract checks it matches and records the price. Then the budget is opened. A bid above it cannot be awarded.
  4. Award. The buyer adds the price points to the fixed quality scores, picks the winner and records the reason on chain, then publishes every offer, price and score.
  5. Delivery and payment. Milestones, review, disputes and withdrawals work as on an open tender. On this tender the contract holds no money, so payment happens outside it.
  1. Bidding. Anyone bids a price up to the ceiling, with the hash of a document describing their offer. A later bid from the same address replaces the earlier one.
  2. Award. After bidding closes, the buyer picks one bid, splits its price into milestones with due dates and records a reason on chain.
  3. Acceptance and funding. The winner checks the milestones and accepts. Accepting pulls the full price into the contract. On this tender that step always fails, because the placeholder token cannot move.
  4. Delivery. The winner submits evidence for each milestone. The reviewer approves it, or either side raises a dispute and the resolver divides that milestone's money.
  5. Payment. Each person withdraws what they are owed. Every step leaves a public event, which is the start of a delivery record.

Before you bid

  • You need an Ethereum-style wallet such as MetaMask or Talisman, set to Kusama Asset Hub, holding a little KSM for two network fees: one to seal, one to open.
  • Leave the price out of your offer document. It is scored before any price is seen.
  • When you seal, the page saves a bid file holding your price and a secret, and keeps a copy in this browser. Keep the file. Without it your bid cannot be opened, and nobody can recover it for you.
  • You can seal again before bids close. Only the last seal counts, so open with the file from the last one.
  • Publish your offer as soon as bids close, then come back while prices are open to open your bid. A seal never opened is not a bid.
  • Your address cannot be the buyer, reviewer or resolver.
  • You need an Ethereum-style wallet such as MetaMask or Talisman, set to Kusama Asset Hub, holding a little KSM for the fee.
  • Your bid must be above zero and no more than the ceiling, and bidding must still be open.
  • Your address cannot be the buyer, reviewer or resolver if you want to be awarded the work.
  • Your terms hash is permanent. Change one character of the document and the hash changes, so keep the exact file you hashed.
  • Nothing here checks who you are. A name, a track record or an on-chain identity are things a real tender would weigh, and how is still being designed.

Why this exists

Kusama's treasury mostly pays for work through single proposals: one team names its own scope, price and timetable, and voters say yes or no. There is no competing offer to compare against, no agreed way to accept the work, and no lasting record of how it went. This page is the bidding end of a prototype that borrows from UK public procurement to test a different route, starting with the security audit that the collaborative decision deposit contracts need.

Read more: the decision deposit thread on the Polkadot forum, the Procurement Act 2023, and the guidance on contract performance notices.
