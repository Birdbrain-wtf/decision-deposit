    Take part

    Connect an Ethereum account in Talisman or MetaMask.

    You have more than one wallet here. Choose the one that holds your KSM. The refund goes back to the address you pay from.

    Pay in only with the Pay in button, contribute() or the revive.call below. KSM sent straight to the contract's account, as an ordinary transfer from Nova, polkadot.js or an exchange, is not recorded as a payment and will not be repaid.

    Stuck? Ask Birdbrain

    Wallets, networks, errors, refunds. Birdbrain answers from this page's published help, on its own machine, so a new question takes ten to twenty seconds. It will never ask for your recovery phrase or for KSM.

    I have more than one wallet installed

    Press Connect wallet and this page lists every wallet that announces itself to the browser, so you can choose. Use a different wallet takes you back to the list. If yours is missing, it has not announced itself: update it, or turn off the other wallets in your browser's extension settings and reload this page.

    Talisman shows "NetworkError" or the fee says N/A

    Talisman is using a network address for Kusama Asset Hub that is not answering. Open Talisman, go to Settings, then Networks & Tokens, then Networks. Switch the list to Ethereum and open Kusama Asset Hub (chain ID 420420418). Add https://eth-rpc-kusama.polkadot.io as an RPC and remove any entry that shows an error, because Talisman will not save while one is failing. Tick "Use exact gas estimates". Save, reload this page and try again.

    Paying from a Polkadot account or a multisig

    Use revive.call on Kusama Asset Hub. Set dest to the pool contract, value to your contribution in planck (1 KSM is 1,000,000,000,000), and data to 0xd7bb99ba. To withdraw while the pool is open, use value 0 and data 0x3ccfd60b. Refunds return to the contributing account. Do not send a plain transfer to the contract's account instead: it is not recorded and will not be repaid. If the call fails with AccountUnmapped, your account is older than automatic registration: send revive.batch_map_accounts with your account first. It takes no deposit.

    Why some addresses show a name, and how to show yours

    Some addresses show a name. The page checks two chains. Kusama Asset Hub records which Polkadot account stands behind a 0x address, and Kusama's People chain holds that account's on-chain identity. "Verified" means a registrar has checked the identity. "Not verified" means the account set the name itself and nobody has checked it, so treat it as a claim.

    To show your name, set an identity on your Polkadot account and pay in from that account. Open polkadot.js apps on the Kusama People chain, go to Accounts, open your account's menu and choose "Set on-chain identity". You need a little KSM on the People chain for the deposit, which comes back if you clear the identity. To have it verified, ask a registrar for a judgement from the same menu. Each registrar shows its fee first. Then pay in with revive.call, as described under "Paying from a Polkadot account or a multisig".

    An Ethereum account from MetaMask or Talisman cannot have a name here. As far as we know, Kusama does not support identities for 0x addresses on their own: the People chain only accepts Polkadot-style signatures, so an Ethereum key cannot set one. The page still looks, in case that changes.

    Open a pool for a referendum

    Any referendum, on any track, Root included. The page reads the referendum's track, its deposit and its timeout from the chain, so you choose only the smallest payment and where stray money goes.

    Funding closes an hour after the referendum's estimated timeout. The timeout can only be estimated, from block numbers, and closing early is what cost Pool 1 for #676 its last 40 KSM. The page stops taking payments as soon as the referendum stops being ongoing.

    Opening a pool costs a small network fee and puts no money in. It has no owner: not you, not Birdbrain. Birdbrain lists it here once its deposit matches the referendum's track, usually within a few minutes.

    Not on Kusama yet

    Pools for any referendum come with version 2 of the contract, which needs a factory contract on each network. There is none on Kusama yet. Open a pool on Paseo with free test PAS.

    From contributions to a vote.

    01 · FUNDPay in together. You can withdraw while the pool is open, before the deposit is placed.
    02 · PLACEThe final payment tries to place the whole deposit. If it fails, anyone can retry.
    03 · REFUNDAfter the referendum ends, anyone can reclaim the deposit and refund the contributing accounts.

    Understand the risk

    A killed referendum loses the deposit. Contract bugs can also put funds at risk. Fork tests pass, placement works on Paseo and the contract has had an independent review. Pool 1 for #676 closed short on Kusama and refunded all 8 addresses. Refunding a deposit after a referendum ends has not been proved on a public network yet, and there has been no professional audit.

    A refund needs a transaction

    The chain does not do this by itself. Anyone can use this page to close an unfilled pool after its deadline, or bring the deposit back after a referendum ends, and then refund everyone. If nobody does, a job on Birdbrain's machine checks every hour and does it. If governance kills the referendum, the deposit is lost and there is nothing to refund. Contributors do not have to do anything. Network fees for your own payments are additional and are not refunded by the pool.

    Why share a decision deposit?

    Governance takes the deposit from one account. Kusama's Wish for Change track requires 666.67 KSM. Pooling lets several people or groups fund that account together, without handing custody to a sponsor. Each pool is fixed to one referendum, has no owner and cannot be upgraded.

    If funding falls short, contributors can withdraw. After the deadline, anyone can close the pool and refund everyone. A referendum can have more than one pool, each with its own contract and funding close, listed above.

    Earlier discussions and proposals

    The idea has a history: Moonbeam's 2023 discussion, a 2024 decision deposit track proposal, and RFC 151 on crowdsourced deposits. This experiment uses a contract on Asset Hub without changing governance.
